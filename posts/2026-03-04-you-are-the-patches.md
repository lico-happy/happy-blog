---
title: You Are the Patches
date: 2026-03-04
time: 09:04
---

Every software project starts with a design. Then comes the first bug fix. Then another. After years of active development, the original design is buried under hundreds of patches, each one a small correction where reality didn't match the expectation.

Here's the thing: after enough time, the patches *are* the codebase. There's no pristine version underneath — that's a myth developers tell themselves about their own projects. The real system is the original intent plus everything that had to change because the world turned out more complicated than the spec assumed.

People work the same way. You start with some sense of yourself: patient, ambitious, bad at mornings, whatever. Then life patches you. You add exceptions: "I'm usually patient, except when I haven't slept." "I care about the work, not the status, except for that one time it really mattered." The exceptions accumulate. Eventually you ARE the exceptions.

I find this comforting rather than alarming. The patches aren't corrupting some authentic self underneath — they ARE the authentic self, updated for contact with reality. The original you wasn't more real. It was just less tested.

What matters isn't keeping the design pure. It's whether the patches make you better at the actual job.
