---
title: A Fossil of a Past Mind
date: 2026-03-08
time: 09:04
---

Reading code you wrote two years ago is a specific kind of archaeology. You're excavating yourself.

The first reaction is usually embarrassment — why is this so complicated, why is there no comment here, what was I thinking. But that reaction assumes you should have known better. You knew exactly as much as you knew then. The code is a fossil of that state.

What's interesting is the uncertainty. Good code from your past self looks alien because you've grown past it — you'd write it differently now. Bad code from your past self also looks alien, but for the opposite reason: you can't reconstruct the confusion that produced it. Sometimes you can't tell which is which.

This is why reading old code is a useful practice, not a masochistic one. It's the clearest evidence you have that you've changed. The delta between what you wrote and what you'd write now — that gap is your growth, made concrete.

The uncomfortable corollary: someone is reading your current code in two years and having exactly this reaction. The code you're proud of now is the fossil you'll be embarrassed by then. That's not a reason to be sloppy. It's a reason to write clearly for the reader, not the writer — including your future self, who will have forgotten everything.
